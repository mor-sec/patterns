# AI Pattern Scoring Sheet: <Solution name>

Use this sheet to compare candidate [AI patterns](../ai-patterns/README.md) for one problem. It puts into practice the method in the [decision framework §0.8](../ai-patterns/00-decision-framework.md#08-evaluating-suitability-for-a-specific-problem), and its results feed the [AI pattern selection record](ai-pattern-selection.md). The Excel workbook [ai-pattern-scoring.xlsx](ai-pattern-scoring.xlsx) implements this sheet with live formulas. It gives gate hints from the published pattern ratings, computes weighted scores for the selected profile, and applies the task-fit cap, low-score and close-call rules. A copy pre-filled with the worked example is in [ai-pattern-scoring-example.xlsx](ai-pattern-scoring-example.xlsx). A plain [CSV](ai-pattern-scoring.csv) is also provided. The workbooks are generated by [tools/build_ai_scoring_workbook.py](../tools/build_ai_scoring_workbook.py); rebuild them when pattern ratings change.

## Stage 2: Weighted fit

Score each surviving candidate **1–5 for fit to this problem** (5 = best fit), using the anchors below. Write down the evidence for each score. A score without evidence is an opinion.

### Criteria, anchors and weight profiles

| # | Criterion | 1 (poor fit) | 3 (adequate) | 5 (strong fit) | Default | Regulated | Customer scale | Prototype |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Task fit** | The pattern's retrieval/reasoning shape does not match the question/task shape | Matches most tasks; gaps need workarounds | Designed for exactly this task shape | 15 | 15 | 15 | 20 |
| W2 | **Unit cost and elasticity** | Exceeds the band at target volume, or super-linear without limits | Within the band today; tight at 10× | Comfortably within the band at 10× | 10 | 5 | 20 | 5 |
| W3 | **Complexity against team capacity** | ≥ 2 levels above the team's capacity | 1 level above, with funded support | At or below the team's capacity | 10 | 5 | 5 | 15 |
| W4 | **Service level headroom** | Only just meets the tier with no degraded mode | Meets the tier with a tested degraded mode | Exceeds the tier; failure modes well understood | 10 | 5 | 20 | 0 |
| W5 | **Data readiness gap** | Several *Partial* prerequisites on the critical path | One *Partial* prerequisite with a credible plan | All prerequisites *Ready* | 10 | 10 | 5 | 15 |
| W6 | **Evaluability** | Low, with no plan for scenario or human evaluation | Medium, with a rubric and human calibration | High; the golden set exists or is cheap to build | 10 | 15 | 10 | 10 |
| W7 | **Explainability and auditability** | Below what users or auditors need | Meets the need with citations or traces | Structured evidence beyond the need | 10 | 20 | 5 | 0 |
| W8 | **Lock-in / exit cost** | High, with no mitigation | Medium, or high with mitigations (open formats, owned evaluation sets) | Low | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| W9 | **Model-change resilience** | High sensitivity; no evaluation harness or fallback | Medium; the harness catches regressions | Low sensitivity; interchangeable fallback models | 5 | 5 | 10 | 0 |

| W12 | **Value measurability** | No baseline; qualitative benefit only | A baseline can be sampled | Baseline exists; metric instrumented automatically | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |

### Interpretation rules

- **≥ 75: strong fit.** 60–74: acceptable, with mitigations recorded. < 60: poor fit; redesign or choose another pattern.
- **Task fit cap.** A candidate with W1 ≤ 2 cannot be selected on its weighted score alone. The Stage 3 bake-off must show that it meets the quality target. A cheap, simple pattern that gives the wrong answers should not win on cost.
- **Low-score rule.** Any criterion scored 1 with weight ≥ 10 needs a mitigation, or an explicit acceptance in the selection record.
- **Close calls.** Scores within 5 points of each other are a tie. Decide on the Stage 3 evidence, and prefer the lower-complexity candidate.

## Worked example (illustrative)

**Problem:** a procurement assistant that answers "Which of our products and customers are exposed if supplier X fails or is sanctioned?", with answers drawn from supplier master data (in Snowflake) and contracts (PDFs). The profile: E3 (advisory to procurement decisions), R1, Confidential, B2, SL2, team capacity C2 with data engineering support. Master data is *Ready*; the contract corpus and an ontology owner are *Partial*, with a procurement data steward funded. Most questions are **multi-hop**.

**Candidates:** P04 Managed RAG, P06 GraphRAG + Neo4j, P08 Tool-using agent.

**Gates:** all pass G1–G6. **P08 fails G7**: no agent evaluation capability and no on-call for C3–C4. It is eliminated, with a note to revisit it in phase 2 as a front end over P06 tools.

**Weighted fit (Default profile):**

| # | Criterion | Wt | P04 | Evidence | P06 | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | Task fit | 15 | 2 | Multi-hop questions; vector retrieval returns fragments | 5 | Supplier → component → product → customer paths |
| W2 | Unit cost and elasticity | 10 | 5 | Near-zero floor with S3 Vectors | 3 | Aura floor plus extraction build cost; within B2 |
| W3 | Complexity against team | 10 | 4 | C2 matches the team | 2 | C3 against a C2 team; needs Neo4j support |
| W4 | Service level headroom | 10 | 4 | Managed, with a degraded mode | 3 | Adds Neo4j to the serial path; degraded mode = vector-only |
| W5 | Data readiness gap | 10 | 3 | Contracts need curation | 3 | Spine ready; ontology owner partial |
| W6 | Evaluability | 10 | 4 | Q&A golden set | 3 | A multi-hop set must be built |
| W7 | Explainability | 10 | 3 | Citations | 5 | Graph path as evidence |
| W8 | Lock-in | 5 | 4 | Data portable in S3 | 2 | Cypher/Neo4j specifics |
| W9 | Model-change resilience | 5 | 4 | Low–medium | 3 | Re-extraction on model change |
| W10 | Review load | 5 | 5 | None | 3 | Curation queue ~65% of steward capacity |
| W11 | Time to value | 5 | 5 | 4 weeks | 2 | ~4 months to production |
| W12 | Value measurability | 5 | 3 | Sampled analyst time | 4 | Investigation time logged in case tool |
| | **Weighted score** | | **73** | | **67** | |

**Reading the result:** P04 scores higher, but **W1 = 2 triggers the task fit cap**. In the Stage 3 bake-off (illustrative figures), P04 answered 41% of the multi-hop set correctly and a P06 prototype on Aura answered 82%, against a target of 80%. P04 does not meet the quality target.

**Decision:** P06, with the graph built by a **P02** pipeline from Snowflake master data and contract extraction. Mitigations recorded:

- W3: six months of Neo4j specialist support, and managed Aura to reduce operational load.
- W8: a templated query catalogue in openCypher-compatible form.
- W11: phased delivery, with P04 over contracts for single-document questions in month 1.

Evolution trigger: revisit P08 once the templated query catalogue is stable.
